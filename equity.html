Inside your equity slice.

Equity isn't one thing. Companies come in sizes, and the mix between them sets how bumpy the ride feels.

Three illustrative mixes

Examples of how the equity part could be split. They are illustrations, not recommendations.

What each size means

Large cap

Big, established companies. Usually smaller falls and slower growth.

Mid cap

Growing companies. More upside, and bigger swings.

Small cap

Small companies. The sharpest gains and the deepest, longest falls.

What can go wrong

Mid and small caps can fall sharply and stay down for years. A bold mix can feel great in a rising market and painful in a falling one. If a 20–30% drop would make you sell, choose a steadier mix. Selling at the bottom is the real loss.

Questions

Index fund or active fund?

An index fund copies a market index at low cost. An active fund has a manager trying to beat it, usually for a higher fee. Both are legitimate. Cost and consistency matter most.

Why does diversification matter?

Spreading across many companies and sizes means one bad result can't sink the whole portfolio.

Which mix should I pick?

Start from your comfort with falls, your time horizon and your age-based mix on the plan page. When unsure, lean steadier.
