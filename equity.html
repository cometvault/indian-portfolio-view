Equity Allocation

Universal directional proportions for the Indian equity market: 50% Mid Cap · 30% Large Cap · 20% Small Cap. These percentages describe how a diversified equity sleeve can look — not which securities to buy or sell.

No stock, index fund or active scheme names are listed. The split is a structural reference commonly discussed for long-term Indian equity exposure. It is not personalised advice and does not constitute a recommendation.

Why this shape is often discussed

Mid Cap (50%)

Historically the segment with a favourable growth-versus-volatility balance for many multi-year periods in India. Forms the growth core of the equity sleeve.

Large Cap (30%)

Provides relative stability, deeper liquidity and lower single-stock concentration risk. Acts as the ballast within equity.

Small Cap (20%)

Offers higher long-term return potential at the cost of larger drawdowns and lower liquidity. Kept as a smaller satellite allocation.

Implementation notes (category level only)

  • The percentages can be realised through passive index funds, active mutual funds, or a combination — scheme names are omitted by design.
  • Periodic rebalancing (e.g., annually or when any sleeve drifts >5 percentage points) keeps the proportions intentional.
  • Investors with shorter horizons or lower risk tolerance may tilt more heavily toward large-cap; those with longer horizons may accept a larger mid/small tilt.
  • Tax, expense ratios and tracking error differ across vehicles; these practical factors are left for individual evaluation.

Index context (illustrative)

Commonly referenced total-return indices for each segment include Nifty 100 / Nifty 50 (large), Nifty Midcap 150 (mid) and Nifty Smallcap 250 (small). Relative performance varies by period; no forward-looking claim is made.
