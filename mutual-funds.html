Mutual Funds

Category-level diversification framework for the Indian mutual-fund landscape. No individual scheme names are shown — only the structural roles different categories play within a long-term allocation.

This page contains no fund recommendations, rankings, or star ratings. It describes universal category characteristics only. Past category performance is not indicative of future results.

Equity categories — role in a portfolio

Large Cap

Focus on the top-ranked companies by market capitalisation. Typically lower volatility relative to broader equity, higher liquidity, and a core role for capital preservation within the equity sleeve.

Mid Cap

Companies ranked outside the largest cohort but still of meaningful size. Historically higher growth potential accompanied by higher drawdowns. Often forms the growth engine of an equity allocation.

Small Cap

Smaller companies with greater variability in earnings and liquidity. Higher long-term return potential historically, offset by deeper and more prolonged drawdowns. Usually a satellite allocation.

Flexi / Multi Cap

Manager discretion across market-cap segments. Can act as a single-fund core for investors who prefer not to rebalance across discrete large/mid/small buckets.

Debt & hybrid categories — stabilisers

Short / Medium Duration Debt

Provide liquidity buffer and lower interest-rate sensitivity. Useful for near-term goals or as a ballast against equity volatility.

Hybrid / Balanced

Combine equity and debt in predefined ratios. Can simplify allocation for investors who want automatic rebalancing between growth and stability.

Illustrative category mix (directional only)

One universal way many long-horizon investors think about proportions — not a prescription:

Equity 40–70%
Debt 20–40%
Gold / Alt 5–15%

Exact weights depend on age, goals, risk capacity and time horizon. The ranges above are educational brackets only.
