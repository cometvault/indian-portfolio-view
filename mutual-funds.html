Basics worth knowing

What is a SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan moves a fixed amount into a fund each month. It builds the habit and averages your buying price over time.

Direct vs regular plans

Direct plans charge a lower expense ratio because no distributor is paid. Regular plans include a distributor's commission. The portfolio is the same.

What is the expense ratio?

The yearly fee a fund takes, shown as a percentage of your money. Small differences compound over many years.

What is the riskometer?

An official label on every scheme showing how risky it is, from low to very high. Match it to how long you can stay invested.

Questions

SIP or lumpsum?

A SIP invests a fixed amount regularly and suits a monthly income. A lumpsum invests everything at once, and the timing risk is yours. Beginners usually find SIPs easier to stick with.

Direct plan or Regular plan?

They hold the same portfolio. The Regular plan includes a distributor commission, so its cost is higher. That gap compounds over the years.

What is NAV, and does a low NAV mean cheap?

NAV is the price of one unit. A low NAV is not cheaper or better. What matters is how the fund performs and what it costs.

Do I need to pick the best fund of the year?

No. Last year's top fund is often not next year's. Choose the right category first, then a consistent, low-cost fund within it.

What if my fund falls in value?

Equity funds do fall, sometimes sharply. Ask whether your goal date or the fund's process has changed. If not, a fall alone is rarely a reason to exit.

How many funds do I need?

Fewer than you think. A few funds that each do a different job are enough. Many funds often just hold the same stocks.
